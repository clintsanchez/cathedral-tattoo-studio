# Target Audience — Cathedral Tattoo Studio

*2026-09-05. ⚠️ **Entirely inferred (④).** No client interview, no customer
interviews, zero reviews on either platform, and no analytics — there is no website.
The evidence base is 97 portfolio photos, the client's own posts ②, and language
lifted from *competitors'* reviews ③.*

> **How to use this.** Treat every persona below as a **hypothesis to test**, not a
> finding. The fastest way to replace this document with something real is to ask

## 01 — Audience overview

The observable customer is not the walk-in flash buyer. Roger's portfolio is
dominated by work that takes planning and multiple sittings: memorial portraits,
religious scenes, dog portraits, script carrying a named person or a Bible verse,
half sleeves and back pieces ①. That work does not get chosen on a Saturday
afternoon because a shop was open — it gets researched, saved to a phone, and
carried around for months before anyone calls.

So the audience is an adult in the Denham Springs / Livingston Parish / Baton Rouge
corridor who has decided on a specific, meaningful piece and is now looking for
someone who will not ruin it. They are choosing an artist, not a shop. Their
frustration with the category is the same one the category creates for itself:
every shop shows a grid of photos and says "custom, all styles, clean and sterile,"
which gives a person deciding on a memorial portrait almost nothing to decide with.

## 02 — Primary audience profile

**Demographics** — inferred from the work and the geography ④
- **Age:** 25–50, centre of mass around 30–40. Large-scale and memorial work skews
  older than flash.
- **Gender:** the portfolio splits fairly evenly. Fine-line, dotwork, florals,
  butterflies and script read to a female audience; large blackwork, tribal and
  religious sleeves read to a male one. **Do not build a single-gender brand.**
- **Location:** Denham Springs, Walker, Watson, Livingston, Central, Baton Rouge.
  Suburban and semi-rural. Car-dependent — a fifteen-minute drive is nothing here.
- **Income:** working and middle. A half sleeve is a real purchase requiring saving,
  which is exactly why it is researched heavily. ⚠️ No pricing data exists.
- **Life stage:** working adults, many with families. The memorial and tribute work
  implies people who have lost someone.

**Psychographics** ④
- **Values:** family, faith or a private spirituality, loyalty, remembrance,
  craftsmanship. This is the Deep South and the portfolio is full of scripture — but
  see §05 on not over-indexing.
- **Self-identity:** "I don't get tattoos casually." Even people with many pieces
  narrate them as chapters, not decoration.
- **Aspiration:** to carry something meaningful, executed well enough to be proud of
  in twenty years.
- **Communities:** church, work crews, gyms, family networks, hunting and outdoors,
  local Facebook groups. Livingston Parish word-of-mouth is dense and fast.

**Behavioral patterns** ④
- **Research-heavy, then decisive.** Weeks or months of looking, then a fast booking.
- **Trigger events:** a death or anniversary; a birth; a milestone or recovery;
  finishing or covering an existing piece; a cover-up of something regretted.
- **Discovery:** Facebook and Instagram first, then Google — "tattoo shops near me",
  "black and grey portrait tattoo Baton Rouge". Then, decisively, a friend's arm.
- **Buying process:** message on social → look at the artist's healed work → ask
  about price and dates → book. **The single largest drop-off in this category is an
  unanswered DM.**

## 03 — Personas

⚠️ Constructed from portfolio evidence, not from interviews. Names invented.

### Danielle, 34 — school aide, Walker
*"I want my mom on my arm and I'm terrified of someone messing it up."*

Lost her mother two years ago. Has one small tattoo from her twenties she doesn't
love. She has been collecting reference photos since the funeral and has looked at
every shop in the parish without messaging one, because a bad portrait is worse than
no portrait.

- **Goals:** a recognisable likeness; an artist who will take the time; to not cry in
  a room full of strangers.
- **Frustrations:** every shop says "custom, all styles"; nobody shows *healed*
  portrait work; she can't tell who actually specialises in faces.
- **Needs:** proof of portrait ability specifically, a private-feeling consult, and
  someone who treats the subject as a person rather than a job.
- **Triggers:** healed black-and-grey portraits, a named consult process, clear
  pricing bands. **Hesitates at:** slow replies, a shop that feels like a party.
- **Channels:** Facebook, Instagram, word of mouth at work and church.
- **How Cathedral solves it:** the portfolio is already dense with memorial and
  portrait work ①; the brand's register treats weight as normal rather than edgy.

### Marcus, 29 — pipefitter, Denham Springs
*"I've got a half sleeve started and I want it finished right."*

Has several pieces, some good, some regretted. Wants a large religious black-and-grey
sleeve and knows enough to judge line quality. Books around a shift rotation and
values an artist who keeps a schedule.

- **Goals:** a cohesive full sleeve; an artist who can draw, not just trace flash; to
  stop collecting mismatched pieces.
- **Frustrations:** shops that overbook and cancel; artists who can't cover or blend
  into existing work; not knowing the real cost of a multi-session project.
- **Needs:** a plan across sessions, honest hours and pricing, dates that hold.
- **Triggers:** big cohesive sleeve work in the portfolio, clear availability.
  **Hesitates at:** vague pricing, an artist who won't commit to a schedule.
- **Channels:** Instagram, Facebook, the crew.
- **How Cathedral solves it:** the published availability calendar ② is exactly the
  scheduling honesty he is looking for, and the portfolio shows large-scale religious
  black and grey ①.

### Hailey, 24 — dental assistant, Baton Rouge
*"I want something fine-line and delicate, and I don't want to feel judged."*

Two small pieces. Wants a fine-line floral or dotwork piece on the ribs or sternum.
Drives fifteen minutes without thinking about it. Chooses on aesthetic and on
atmosphere — the shop has to feel clean, calm and unintimidating.

- **Goals:** delicate work that ages well; a comfortable experience; something that
  photographs well.
- **Frustrations:** heavy-handed artists who blow out fine lines; shops that feel
  like a boys' club; being talked out of what she wants.
- **Needs:** visible fine-line and dotwork specialisation, a calm room, a woman-safe
  atmosphere.
- **Triggers:** dotwork and fine-line portfolio depth, clean interior photos.
  **Hesitates at:** an aggressive or gatekeeping vibe.
- **Channels:** Instagram and TikTok primarily. ✅ **Correction 2026-09-05: this
  persona is reachable today.** Roger's `@rc_tattoos` has 6,667 followers and 2,029
  posts (`brand/naming.md`). The reach exists; it sits on the artist account rather
  than the studio one.
- **How Cathedral solves it:** the fine-line dotwork body of work is genuinely strong
  ①, and the gold-on-black brand reads considered rather than aggressive.

## 04 — Audience language

⚠️ **No Cathedral customer language exists** — zero reviews, thin comment volume. The
phrases below come from *competitors'* review text ③ and from the client's own posts
②. Replace with real language the moment reviews start.

- **Problem, as they say it:** "I want to get my [person] tattooed and I want it done
  right." · "I've got one I need covered up." · "I want to finish my sleeve." ④
- **Desired outcome:** "it looks exactly like her" · "he listened to what I wanted" ③
  (recurring theme in Faithful's reviews) · "clean lines" · "it healed perfect."
- **Justifying a competitor:** "they've been there forever" (Burning Lotus, ~168
  reviews) ③ · "it's not the cheapest but it's worth it" ③ (recurring in Faithful's
  reviews — **this market already accepts paying more for quality**) · "they take
  walk-ins" (Safe Voyage) ③.
- **The client's own words ②:** "now booking", "available", "bigger location".

**Brand-building implication.** Two things carry: *listening* and *healed results*.
The category sells the tattoo; this audience is buying the certainty that the person
holding the machine understood them. Say almost nothing about equipment and almost
everything about the conversation and the outcome. And use the plain words the
audience uses — "black and grey", "cover-up", "sleeve", "portrait" — rather than
brand-poetic language.

## 05 — Who this brand is not for

- **The walk-in impulse buyer.** Cathedral is appointment-led and, until the move,
  cannot take walk-ins at all. Safe Voyage owns that customer 200 yards away.
- **The lowest-price shopper.** ⚠️ Cathedral has no price advantage and shouldn't try
  to build one; the local evidence says quality buyers already pay up.
- **The party/bachelorette crowd.** Fun, fast, matching tattoos — the brand's whole
  register argues against it.
- **Anyone seeking hate imagery or extremist iconography.** Worth stating explicitly
  in shop policy, given a gothic and religious visual language can attract
  misreadings.
- **⚠️ Not *only* Christians.** The obvious trap. The portfolio is heavy with
  religious work, but it also holds Capricorn sea-goats, Pegasus, Zeus, octopuses,
  Deadpool and a Guy Fawkes mask ①. Faithful already owns the explicitly Christian
  seat with real reviews (`competitors.md`). Cathedral should read as *reverent*, not
  as *denominational* — reverence includes the customer getting their dog memorialised.

## 06 — Implications

**Messaging** — lead with the weight of the work and evidence of skill on the
specific thing they want. Portrait people need portrait proof; fine-line people need
fine-line proof. Segment the portfolio by what the piece *is*, not by style jargon.

**Channel** — ⚠️ **Revised 2026-09-05.** Instagram, not Facebook, is the real
channel: `@rc_tattoos` carries 6,667 followers and 2,029 posts against the studio
Facebook page's 217 ① (`brand/naming.md`). Plan around Instagram first and treat
Facebook as secondary. TikTok remains unverified. Google is the intent channel and Cathedral is invisible
there — no website, no reviews. ⚠️ Local search work is gated on the address.

**Voice** — plain, warm, direct, unhurried. Match the register of the client's own
posts ②, not the gothic drama of the mark. Never intimidating, never salesy, never
precious about the art.

**Experience** — reply fast (the DM is the funnel); make consults feel private; show
healed work; publish real availability; be explicit about the multi-session plan and
what it costs. Every one of these is an operational habit, not a brand asset — which
is why they are worth writing down before the roster grows.
