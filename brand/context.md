## Positioning
- **Differentiation**: **reverence and commissioned work.**
  ✅ **Adopted by BSC as the working differentiator (Clint, 2026-09-05.)** Origin is
  still ④ inference, not a client statement — so it may guide all internal work and
  shape drafts, but **it is not published as Roger's own claim until he confirms it**
  (R-18). The reasoning: the brand is ecclesiastical and the work matches
  it. A gothic cathedral mark over a portfolio heavy in religious and memorial
  black-and-grey is a rare alignment — most shops wear a brand that has nothing to do
  with what they tattoo. That congruence is the asset, and it is defensible because
  it is true.
- **Competitors**: Tattootopia (2320 Florida Ave SW — one mile up the same street,
  and Roger's prior shop ⚠️ also a BSC pro-bono client), Faithful Tattoo Studio
  (105 N Range Ave), Burning Lotus, Safe Voyage, Cipher ③. Full analysis pending
  `competitor-branding`.
- **Market Position**: **premium.** ✅ Set by Clint 2026-09-05.
  The top of the local market, not a mid-market shop that charges a bit more. Cathedral
  is the considered, expensive-feeling choice: appointment-led, selective, unhurried.
  Supported by local review evidence ③ that this market already pays above the cheapest
  option (Faithful's reviews repeatedly say "not the cheapest but worth it").

  🚨 **The governing rule: premium is expressed, never claimed.**
  Cathedral has zero reviews, no published prices and a four-month-old entity. A shop
  in that position that *announces* it is premium reads as pretension, and it would
  land as a direct answer to Tattootopia's "premier" and "utopia" — the one fight
  `positioning.md` says not to pick. Premium therefore shows up in **restraint,
  selectivity, craft evidence and pace**, never in adjectives.
  - ❌ Never: "premium", "luxury", "high-end", "elite", "exclusive", "bespoke",
    "the best in the parish". Add these to the `voice.md` never-say list.
  - ✅ Instead: fewer, larger pieces of work shown. More black, less content. Named
    artists. Visible process. Waiting lists rather than discounts.

  **What premium now forbids operationally:**
  - **No discounting, no promos, no flash-sale urgency.** ⚠️ Tension to resolve: their
    grand opening was a *flash event* ① and the availability posts function as
    promos ②. Flash days are a volume mechanic and sit awkwardly under premium.
    Not a blocker — but decide deliberately whether flash days continue, rather than
    letting the two positions collide in public.
  - **A deposit and a consult should gate booking.** That is the clearest premium
    signal available to a tattoo studio and it costs nothing.
  - **Do not chase walk-ins as a growth strategy** even once the new location allows
    them. Convenience is Safe Voyage's territory 200 yards away, and premium and
    walk-in volume pull against each other.

  ✅ **R-07 answered 2026-09-05 (Clint): there is no shop minimum.** The studio is open
  and trading.

  🚨 **This is the largest single threat to the premium position, and it is worth
  saying plainly.** Zero reviews is a problem that time fixes. No minimum is a problem
  only a decision fixes, and it is a decision that has already been made by default.

  **Two different things must not be confused:**
  - **No published price list — fine, and arguably premium.** Serious custom studios
    quote per piece. Cathedral should not publish a rate card.
  - **No minimum at all — not premium.** A minimum is not a price, it is a *filter*. It
    is what stops the calendar filling with £40 walk-in butterflies that consume chair
    time, generate no portfolio, and quietly reposition the shop as cheap. A studio
    that will tattoo anything at any price is, by definition, a volume shop. That is a
    perfectly good business — it is simply not the one the rest of this package
    describes.

  **Recommendation (BSC): institute a minimum.** It is the cheapest, fastest premium
  lever available and it is operational rather than cosmetic. Local anchor ③: a
  comparable Baton Rouge shop runs **$100 minimum / $150 hourly**.
  - **$100** — market-standard. Filters the smallest work without deterring anyone
    serious. The safe choice.
  - **$150** — sits deliberately above the local floor and signals the position
    honestly. The choice that matches the brand.
  - Pair it with a **deposit** taken at booking and applied to the piece. The deposit
    does more work than the number: it converts enquiries into commitments and stops
    no-shows, which is what actually costs a small studio money.

  ⚠️ **Pricing is Roger's decision, not ours.** Our job is to say what the brand
  requires and let him weigh it against his cost base and chair time.

  **If he will not set a minimum**, premium has to be carried entirely by other
  mechanics, and this must be deliberate rather than accidental:
  consult-gated booking · deposits · project-only work · declining small flash ·
  publishing fewer, larger pieces · waiting lists rather than availability.
  ⚠️ It is achievable, but it is harder, slower, and every one of those has to hold.
