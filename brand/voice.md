# Brand Voice — Cathedral Tattoo Studio

*2026-09-05. **v2, 2026-10-11.** ⚠️ Proposed (④), but **now live**: the owner
responses and posts on the Google Business Profile are written in this voice ①, so
there are real samples to hold new copy against. Grounded in the client's own plain
posts ② and derived from `positioning.md`.*

> **v2 changes:** walk-ins may be stated (§08) · reviews may be quoted, with rules
> (§09, new) · "Selective" is softened so it never promises a refusal the shop does not
> practise (§03) · one new quality, **At ease**, taken from customers' own words (§03).

> **The one rule that governs this whole document.** The *mark* is gothic. The
> *voice* is not. Cathedral's logo is a blackletter cathedral facade in gold, and the
> instinct it creates — writing sepulchral, ornate, faux-liturgical copy — is wrong
> and would be the single fastest way to make this brand ridiculous. Roger's own
> posts are plain announcements ②. The gothic does the atmosphere; the words do the
> work. **Let the logo be dramatic so the writing doesn't have to be.**

## 01 — Voice overview

Cathedral speaks like a skilled tradesperson who takes the work seriously and has no
need to perform. Plain, warm, unhurried, specific. It explains what a piece will take
and what it will cost, it answers questions without condescension, and it never
oversells, because the portfolio is doing that job already. When the subject is heavy
— a memorial, a name, a face — it does not get poetic; it gets careful. It treats the
customer as an adult making a considered decision, which is exactly what they are.

**Voice Essence.** *Cathedral sounds like a craftsman with steady hands — plain,
warm, and unhurried.*

## 02 — Tone dimensions

**Formality — casual side, but never sloppy.**
Contractions yes, slang no, profanity never. Writes like a person talking across a
counter, not like a brochure.
> "Send me a reference photo and roughly where you want it, and I'll tell you what it'll take."

**Energy — calm.** The category shouts; Cathedral doesn't. No exclamation marks, no
urgency manufactured out of nothing. The only real scarcity is the calendar, and that
scarcity speaks for itself. ⚠️ **Premium sharpens this into a hard rule: no discount
language, no promo language, no countdowns, no "spots going fast".** A brand that
discounts is telling you its prices were negotiable.
> "October is open. November is filling."

**Humour — dry and rare.** Warmth carries the personality, not jokes. Never jokes
about pain, regret, or a customer's idea.

**Expertise — accessible, never gatekeeping.** Cathedral knows more than the customer
and never makes them feel it. It explains black and grey versus colour, or why a
fine-line piece on a rib ages the way it does, in the customer's own words.
> "Fine lines on the ribs soften over the years. It's normal. Here's how I plan for it."

**Warmth — close, but not familiar.** Warm toward the person, restrained about the
subject. Never "so sorry for your loss" as a form phrase. Attention is the warmth.

## 03 — Voice qualities

**Plain**
Says the ordinary thing in the ordinary word. Cathedral never reaches for elevated
language, because elevated language around tattoos reads as marketing and this
audience is scanning for marketing.
- **Do:** "It'll take about three sittings."
- **Don't:** "Your journey will unfold across a series of sessions."

**Specific**
The antidote to a category built on "custom, all styles." Specificity is Cathedral's
proof, and it is free.
- **Do:** "Black and grey portraits, religious pieces, fine-line and dotwork."
- **Don't:** "All styles welcome."

**Careful**
The register for weight. When the piece carries a person, the writing slows down and
gets concrete. No metaphor, no uplift.
- **Do:** "Bring the clearest photo you have of her. Face-on is best, but I can work with most things."
- **Don't:** "We'll transform your grief into something beautiful."

**Honest about limits**
Cathedral says what it cannot do, and what it does not yet know. This is a competitive
weapon in a category of unfalsifiable superlatives, and for a shop this new it is also
simply necessary.
- **Do:** "That's a cover-up, and it'll need to be bigger and darker than what's there. Here's why."
- **Don't:** "Anything is possible."

**Unhurried**
Never pressures. The booking mechanic is availability, not urgency.
- **Do:** "Think about it. The dates will still be there tomorrow."
- **Don't:** "Book now — spots going fast!"

**Selective** ⚠️ *revised v2*
Premium reads through what Cathedral declines, not what it offers. ⚠️ But the shop
takes walk-ins and has no minimum, so copy must **not invent a refusal**. Selectivity
is expressed by being specific about what the work is, never by claiming limits on
intake that nobody at the studio has set.
- **Do:** "Large pieces get planned across sittings, so they get the time they need."
- **Don't:** "Booking all styles, all sizes, any time."
- **Don't (v2):** "I take a limited number of large pieces at a time." ❌ Unverified.
  It was the v1 example and it claims a policy Roger has never stated.

**At ease** ✅ *new in v2, from customers ①*
The quality people actually wrote reviews about. The voice lowers the stakes: it tells
a nervous person what will happen and that they can ask anything. It never says
"don't worry" and never performs friendliness.
- **Do:** "First one? Tell us. We'll walk you through it before anything starts."
- **Do:** "Being nervous about it is more common than people let on." *(live, GBP
  owner response)*
- **Don't:** "Our friendly team will make you feel right at home!"

**Attributive**
Names the artist who did the work, every time. This is the architecture in
`architecture.md` showing up in the copy.
- **Do:** "Sleeve by Roger Cazares."
- **Don't:** "Another one from the Cathedral team."

## 04 — Vocabulary

**Words we use.** Book · booking · sitting · session · consult · piece · work ·
healed · cover-up · black and grey · fine line · dotwork · lettering · script ·
sleeve · half sleeve · back piece · portrait · memorial · reference · placement ·
line work · shading · touch-up · aftercare · open dates · available.

Plain trade language, and the same words the audience uses ④.

**Words we avoid.** 🚨 **Premium-position additions (2026-09-05): premium · luxury ·
high-end · elite · exclusive · bespoke · boutique · upscale · discerning · indulge.**
Cathedral *is* premium and must never *say* it — the position is expressed through
restraint and craft evidence, and claiming it from a shop with zero reviews reads as
pretension (`context.md`).
Also: ink (as a noun: "get some ink") · inked · body art (fine in legal
and regulatory contexts, wrong in marketing) · parlor · premier · utopia · best ·
world-class · unrivalled · state-of-the-art · passion for the craft · bring your
vision to life · your body is a canvas · no regrets · journey · transform · elevate ·
curated · bespoke · luxury · indulge · pamper · unleash · epic · insane · fire ·
sick · "we don't just do tattoos, we…".

⚠️ **"Premier" and "utopia" are Tattootopia's words** (`competitors.md`). Using them
would read as an answer to the shop up the street, which is exactly the fight to avoid.

**Brand-specific language**
- **"Cathedral"** on second reference. Never "CTS" in prose — that mark is a graphic
  device only (`architecture.md`).
- **Customers are "clients"**, or just "you". Never "guests", never "the Cathedral
  family", never a coined community name.
- **Artists are named people.** "Roger Cazares", then "Roger". Never "our team of
  talented artists".
- **The calendar is "open dates"** — Roger's own phrasing ②. Keep it.

## 05 — Writing style rules

- **Sentence length:** short to medium. Mix, but when the subject gets heavy, go
  shorter. Long sentences read as evasion.
- **Paragraphs:** four lines maximum (BSC house rule).
- **Em dashes: banned in client-facing copy.** BSC house convention, and they are a
  strong AI-detection signal. Use a comma, a full stop, or a colon.
  ⚠️ *These internal brand documents use them freely; the rule binds published copy —
  site, social, email, print.*
- **Punctuation:** no exclamation marks in shop-level copy. Ellipses banned. Semicolons
  are fine but rarely needed at this register.
- **Capitalisation:** sentence case everywhere, headlines included. Title Case Reads
  Like An Ad. The only exception is the wordmark itself.
- **Numbers:** numerals for prices, hours, dates, sessions and sizes ("3 sittings",
  "$150"). Spell out one through nine in ordinary prose.
- **Contractions:** yes, always. "It'll", "you'll", "don't". Their absence is what
  makes tattoo-shop copy sound like a legal notice.
- **Voice:** active. "Roger drew this", not "this was drawn by".
- **Humour:** allowed, dry, sparing. Never at the customer's expense, never about pain.
- **Second person.** Write to one person, not to a market. "You", not "our clients".

## 06 — Channel adaptations

Voice is constant. Tone flexes.

| Channel | Adjustment | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Calmest, fewest words. The work carries it | "Commissioned tattoo work in Denham Springs." ✅ address confirmed, state it plainly |
| Service / style pages | Most specific. Explain process, sittings, healing | "Most half sleeves run 3 to 4 sittings, spaced a few weeks apart." |
| Portfolio captions | Shortest. Name the piece, name the artist | "Memorial portrait, forearm. Roger Cazares." |
| Facebook / Instagram | Warmest and most direct. Match Roger's existing register ② | "Open dates for October are up. Message me to get on the books." |
| DMs and texts | Fastest and most human. This is the real funnel | "Yeah, I can do that. Send me the photo and I'll give you a price." |
| Email | Slightly more structured, same plainness. No newsletter voice | "Here's your appointment, and what to do the day before." |
| Aftercare | Instructional and unambiguous. Numbered steps. No personality | "Leave the wrap on for 2 hours. Wash with unscented soap." |
| Booking forms / UI | Short, literal labels. No cleverness | "What do you want tattooed?" not "Tell us your vision" |

## 07 — Before and after

**Before:** "Welcome to Cathedral Tattoo Studio, Livingston Parish's premier
destination for world-class custom body art. Our award-winning artists bring your
vision to life in a clean, safe and sterile environment."
**After:** "Custom tattoo work in Denham Springs. Black and grey, fine line,
lettering and cover-ups. Walk in, or book ahead." *(v2: "Booking by appointment" removed.)*
**Why:** the original makes four claims Cathedral is not allowed to make (premier,
award-winning, sterility, and an implied establishment) and one it doesn't need. The
rewrite says what it is, what it does, and how to get it.

**Before:** "Embark on your tattoo journey with us! ✨ Our passionate artists are
ready to transform your ideas into stunning works of art!!"
**After:** "Tell me what you're thinking about and I'll tell you what it'll take."
**Why:** removes the exclamation marks, the journey, and the transformation, and
replaces them with the sentence a nervous first-timer actually needs to read.

**Before:** "We specialise in helping you honour your loved ones through beautiful
memorial tattoos that keep their memory alive forever."
**After:** "Memorial portraits. Bring the clearest photo you have, and we'll talk
through placement and size before anything gets drawn."
**Why:** the original sells grief back to the grieving. The rewrite respects it by
being useful.

**Before:** "🔥🔥 LIMITED SPOTS LEFT!! DM NOW before they're GONE!!"
**After:** "Open dates for October are up. First come, first served."
**Why:** the scarcity is real, so it needs no amplification. Manufactured urgency is
the fastest way to look like every other shop.

**Before:** "Our state-of-the-art facility maintains the highest standards of
sterilisation and safety, exceeding all industry regulations."
**After:** *(say nothing yet)*
**Why:** ⚠️ **guardrail 4.** No sterility, licensing or compliance claim until Roger
provides the permit numbers in writing. This is the rewrite that has no on-brand
version — it has an on-brand *silence*.

## 08 — Things we never say

- **Any ownership claim** for anyone, until the LLC question is settled (guardrail 1).
- **Any premium adjective.** See vocabulary above. This is the most likely accidental
  breach now that the position is premium.
- ~~Any address, until it is confirmed.~~ ✅ **Cleared.** 1318 Florida Ave SW may be
  published. ⚠️ Never publish the Bluebonnet Blvd filing address as the studio, and do
  not announce the move until there is a date.
- **Any tenure claim.** No "years of experience", no "established", no "since". The
  business is four months old and Roger's personal years are unknown (guardrail 3).
- **Any sterility, licensing, safety or compliance claim** (guardrail 4).
- **Any inflated review claim.** Reviews exist (6, 5.0 on 2026-10-11) and may be
  quoted under §09. Never "top-rated", "loved by", "5-star studio" or a rounded count.
- ~~"Walk-ins welcome"~~ ✅ **Allowed since 2026-09-19.** Walk-ins are live. State it
  as a fact: "Walk in, or book ahead." ❌ Still never "appointment only" or "by
  appointment", which are now false, and never walk-ins as a headline or a promo.
- **Any invented differentiator** (guardrail 7).
- **Any mention of Tattootopia**, or any comparison to another local shop.
- Hollow hype: world-class, premier, unrivalled, best in the parish, game-changing.
- Over-apologetic filler: "sorry for the inconvenience", "just checking in".
- Corporate padding: "reach out", "circle back", "at your convenience", "we pride
  ourselves on".
- Anything that jokes about pain, regret, drunk decisions, or a customer's idea.
- Anything implying a tattoo is an impulse worth having.

## 09 — Reviews: quoting them and answering them (new in v2)

**Quoting.** Verbatim, first name, "Google review". Do not fix their grammar or cut a
sentence in half to improve it. One quote per surface is plenty. Prefer the quotes
about ease and about a named artist; skip "price friendly" and "best tattoos you can
ever get", which pull the brand into price and superlatives.

**Answering.** The live responses ① are the standard. What they do, and should keep
doing:
- Open with thanks and the first name. No "Dear", no "valued customer".
- Pick up **one specific thing** the reviewer said and add something true to it.
- Name the artist when the customer did.
- Two or three sentences. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no request for a referral.
- Admit what is true: "You're right that we're still getting the place sorted."

> "Thanks Kameryn. We'll pass this on to Cleo. Somebody coming back for every piece
> says more than anything we could put on a page." — live, and exactly right.

⚠️ **One thing to stop:** "Come back when you're ready for the second" and "When
you're ready for the next one, you know where we are" close two of five responses.
Fine once. As a pattern it becomes a sales line, which the voice does not have.

**Negative reviews** (none yet, write the rule now): answer within a day, plainly,
without defending. Say what happened if you know, offer to fix it by phone, give the
number. Never argue the facts in public.
