## Voice summary

Cathedral sounds like a skilled tradesperson talking across a counter. The work is taken seriously and nothing is performed. It says what a piece will take, answers the question that was asked, and never oversells, because the photographs are doing that job already. When the subject is heavy, a memorial, a name, a face, it does not get poetic. It gets careful. It treats the reader as an adult making a considered decision. It tells a nervous person what will happen and that they can ask anything. Praise comes from customers, in their words, with their names on it.

**The voice in one sentence**

> A craftsman with steady hands: plain, warm and unhurried.

**Three rules**

1. **The mark is gothic. The voice is not.** The logo is a blackletter cathedral in gold. The writing beside it is ordinary words in ordinary order. Let the logo be dramatic so the writing does not have to be.
2. **Show the weight, never claim it.** The position is expressed through restraint, craft evidence and pace. No premium word is ever written, and no superlative.
3. **Name the artist.** Every piece of work is credited to the person who made it, by name.

**The test for any sentence:** could any tattoo shop say it? Then cut it or make it specific. Second test: would it sound right said out loud across a counter, to one person? If it would sound like a brochure or a sermon, rewrite it.

## Core voice traits

Eight qualities. Every "do" is a draft.

### Plain

What it means: the ordinary thing in the ordinary word. Elevated language around tattoos reads as marketing, and this reader is scanning for marketing.

- **Do:** "It'll take about three sittings."
- **Don't:** "Your journey will unfold across a series of sessions."

`CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`: any number of sittings. The form of the sentence is the point; the number comes from the artist.

### Specific

What it means: name the work. Specificity is the proof in a category built on "custom, all styles".

- **Do:** "Black and grey portraits, religious pieces, fine line and dotwork."
- **Don't:** "All styles welcome."

### Careful

What it means: the register for weight. When the piece carries a person, the writing slows down and gets concrete. No metaphor, no uplift, no form phrase of sympathy.

- **Do:** "Bring the clearest photo you have of her. Face-on is best, but I can work with most things."
- **Don't:** "We'll transform your grief into something beautiful."

### Honest about limits

What it means: say what cannot be done, and what is not yet known. A limit said plainly reads as care.

- **Do:** "That's a cover-up, and it'll need to be bigger and darker than what's there. Here's why."
- **Don't:** "Anything is possible."

### Unhurried

What it means: no pressure, ever. No urgency, no countdown, no offer.

- **Do:** "Think about it. The dates will still be there tomorrow."
- **Don't:** "Book now, spots going fast!"

### Selective

What it means, as revised: the studio is known by what its work is, not by a claim about who it turns away. The studio takes walk-ins and has no minimum, so copy never invents a refusal, a limit on intake or a waiting list. Selectivity is expressed by being specific about the work and by showing fewer pieces, larger.

- **Do:** "Large pieces get planned across sittings, so they get the time they need."
- **Don't:** "Booking all styles, all sizes, any time."
- **Don't:** "I take a limited number of large pieces at a time." Nobody at the studio has said so.

### At ease

What it means: the quality customers wrote reviews about. The voice lowers the stakes. It tells a nervous person what will happen and that they can ask anything. It never says "don't worry" and never performs friendliness.

- **Do:** "First one? Tell us. We'll walk you through it before anything starts."
- **Do:** "Being nervous about it is more common than people let on." (Live, from a reply to a Google review.)
- **Don't:** "Our friendly team will make you feel right at home!"

### Attributive

What it means: the artist who did the work is named, every time, by real name. Never "the team".

- **Do:** "Sleeve by Roger Cazares."
- **Don't:** "Another one from the Cathedral team."

Roger Cazares may be named. `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`: every use of the names Mikayla Paul and Cleo. Neither has agreed to be named in marketing, and Cleo's surname is unknown.

## Writing rules

- Use active voice. "Roger drew this", not "this was drawn by".
- Address one person: "you". Not "our clients", not "guests", not a market.
- Prefer concrete language: write what happens, not the benefit.
- Keep sentences short to medium. When the subject gets heavy, go shorter. Long sentences read as evasion.
- Paragraphs are four lines at most.
- **Use contractions: yes, always.** "It'll", "you'll", "don't". Their absence makes shop copy sound like a legal notice.
- **Use first person: yes.** "I" on an artist's own channel and in a signed reply. "We" for the studio. Never the third person about the studio in its own copy. `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`: who writes as Cathedral. Until the studio answers, shop pages say "we" and artist pages say "I" only inside a quotation from the artist.
- **Use slang: no.** No "fire", "sick", "insane", "epic".
- **Use humour: dry and rare.** Warmth carries the personality, not jokes. Never about pain, regret, a drunk decision or a customer's idea.
- **Use profanity: never.**
- **Use emoji: never.** Not on the website, in Google posts, in review replies or in graphics.
- **Use exclamation marks: never.** Not one, anywhere in the studio's own voice.
- **Use ellipses: never.**
- **Use trade terms: yes, the plain ones, explained when the reader is new.** "Sitting", "stencil", "touch-up", "healed". Explain in the reader's words: "Fine lines on the ribs soften over the years. It's normal."
- No dashes used as punctuation. Use a full stop, a comma or a colon. This includes ranges: "10am to 6pm", "3 to 4 sittings".
- No capitals for emphasis. No bold inside a sentence to sell.
- Sentence case everywhere, headlines included. Title case reads like an advert. The only capitals are the ones the heading typeface sets by style (file 3).
- No rhetorical question with the brand as the answer. A question is fine when it is the reader's real question: "First one?"
- No hashtags stuffed into copy. On Instagram and Facebook, at most three, plain and true, after the caption. None on Google posts.
- Semicolons are allowed and rarely needed.
- Every factual claim has a source someone could check. If it does not, leave a bracketed note for the editor and do not fill the gap.

### The "never liturgical" rule

The logo creates one strong temptation: to write to match it. Sepulchral, ornate, church-flavoured copy is the single fastest way to make this brand ridiculous, and it would also read as a claim about faith that nobody at the studio has made.

"Cathedral" is the studio's name. It is not a theme.

- **Never these words, in any form, about the studio, the work or the client:** sanctuary, sacred, hallowed, holy, ritual, rite, congregation, pilgrimage, devotion, blessed, reverent, worship, altar, relic, sanctum, communion, confession, sermon, gospel, anointed, consecrated, divine, eternal.
- **Never gothic prose:** shadows, darkness, souls, blood, bone, the grave, "etched in eternity", "ink and iron", archaic spellings, "thee" and "thou".
- **Never a play on the name:** "your skin, our cathedral", "enter the cathedral", "built to last like stone", "a temple of". No second coinage for a service, a room or an event.
- **Never a claim about religion:** no "Christian", no "faith-based", no denominational framing, and no scripture used as marketing copy, in a headline, a caption or a graphic.
- **Religious tattoos are named plainly, as work.** "Christ and dove, forearm. Roger Cazares." The caption never comments on belief, and words from a tattoo are never lifted into a headline.
- **The same goes for design language in copy.** Do not describe the brand to the public as "gothic", "dark" or "ecclesiastical". Those are words for this kit, not for a customer.

The test: take the logo away. The sentence should still sound like a person in Denham Springs who is good at their job.

## Headline style

Plain statements in sentence case that say what the work is, where the studio is, or the reader's real question. No superlative, no pun, no exclamation mark, no rhetorical hook, no church vocabulary. A hero headline is 8 words at most, because the heading typeface wants short lines; a social headline is 6 words at most. A full stop at the end, or none.

Examples (all drafts):

- "Custom tattoo work in Denham Springs."
- "Black and grey, fine line, lettering and cover-ups"
- "Memorial portraits"
- "First one? Tell us."
- "What happens at a first sitting"
- "Walk in, or book ahead." (as a line in the hours block, never as the hero)

`CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`: the proposed tagline, "Work worth carrying". It goes on nothing permanent until the studio approves it. The proposed descriptor is "Custom tattoo work in Denham Springs. Walk in, or book ahead."

## CTA style

Fixed wording, everywhere. The phone is a button and not a bare link.

Preferred:

- **"See the work"** (primary)
- **"Call or text (225) 301-9527"** (secondary, linked `tel:+12253019527`; in a narrow header it may shorten to "Call or text")
- **"Check open dates"** (third, only where real dates are shown)
- A text link that names its destination: "See the fine line work"
- In a sentence: "Call or text (225) 301-9527, or walk in."

Avoid:

- "Book now", "Book online", "Book your appointment" (no booking form exists)
- "Get inked", "Get tatted", "Claim your spot"
- "Contact us", "Learn more", "Submit", "Click here", "Reach out"
- "Free consultation", "Get a quote", "Request pricing" (no price is discussed in marketing)
- "Email us" (no email is printed)
- "Visit our website" (none exists)
- Any urgency: "Don't wait", "Limited spots", "Before they're gone"

## Review responses

Reviews are quoted and answered under one standard. The replies already live on the Google profile are the reference.

### Quoting a review

- Verbatim. Do not fix the grammar, the capitals or the punctuation, and do not cut a sentence in half to improve it.
- First name, then "Google review". No surname, no initial, no photograph, no star row.
- Only from the approved list in files 5 and 6. One quotation per view.
- Prefer the quotations about ease and about a named artist.
- Never a quotation from a reviewer who shares a surname with a member of the studio. Never "price friendly". Never "Best tattoos you can ever get".
- Set in the body typeface, upright, never the heading typeface (file 3).
- The rating appears only as the dated line: "5.0 on Google from 6 reviews (October 11, 2026)".

### Answering a review

What the live replies do, and should keep doing:

1. Open with thanks and the first name. No "Dear", no "valued customer".
2. Pick up **one specific thing** the reviewer said and add something true to it.
3. Name the artist when the customer did.
4. Two or three sentences. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no request for a referral, no keyword.
5. Admit what is true. A live reply does exactly this: "You're right that we're still getting the place sorted."

That last line is live as a reply and is right there. It is not reused in marketing, where nothing is said about the premises beyond the address.

**One habit to stop.** Two of the live replies close with a line inviting the next tattoo: "Come back when you're ready for the second" and "When you're ready for the next one, you know where we are". Once is fine. As a pattern it becomes a sales line, and the voice does not have one.

A reply in the standard (draft, for a review that thanks a named artist for patience):

> Thanks Keely. We'll make sure Roger sees this. Taking the time over the drawing is most of the job, and it's good to hear it showed.

### A critical review

None exists yet. The rule is written now.

- Answer within a day.
- Plainly, without defending. Say what happened, if it is known.
- Offer to put it right by phone and give the number: (225) 301-9527.
- Never argue the facts in public. Never "we're sorry you feel that way". Never a public discount. Never ask for the review to be changed.
- Under 80 words.
