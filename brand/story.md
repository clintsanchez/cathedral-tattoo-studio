# Brand Story — Cathedral Tattoo Studio

## ⛔ Not written. This is a scaffold and an interview guide.

*2026-09-05. Deliberately incomplete.*

The `brand-story` skill asks for a 600–900 word origin narrative, a 150–200 word
short version, and a one-line origin. **All three are blocked, and writing them
anyway would be the single most damaging thing in this whole brand package.**

Every other document here is inference built on observable evidence and labelled as
such. A brand story is different in kind: it makes first-person factual claims about
a real person's motives, history and decisions. There is no tier ④ version of "why
Roger started this." Invent it and it becomes About-page copy, then GBP copy, then
something Roger has to either endorse or publicly correct.

## Why it is blocked — four reasons, any one of which is enough

1. **Ownership is unresolved.** The LLC names Mikayla Paul as sole member; Clint
   states Roger is the owner (`06-Reports/records-check.md`). A founding story has to
   say who founded it. Cathedral cannot answer that question today, and the skill's
   own template opens with the founder's realisation and decision. Guardrail 1.

2. **The obvious narrative arc is off-limits.** The natural story — an artist leaves
   the shop up the street and opens his own — runs straight through Tattootopia,
   which is Roger's prior shop, one mile away, **and a BSC pro-bono client**. The
   positioning explicitly refuses a prior-shop chapter (`positioning.md` §06), and the
   conflict is unresolved (`competitors.md` §07). ⚠️ Clint decision required.

3. **The skill requires a villain, and Cathedral's only available villain is a
   neighbour.** "Name the villain" is one of the skill's story principles. In this
   category the honest villains are complacency and shops that rush meaningful work.
   Aimed at a parish with five named competitors one mile apart, that reads as a
   direct shot at identifiable local businesses. It needs to be aimed at an *attitude*
   and Roger needs to be the one who aims it.

4. **Nothing is known.** Not why he started, not what he did before, not what he
   wanted that he could not get elsewhere, not the moment he decided. Zero client
   input exists. The story is the one document that cannot be reconstructed from a
   Google listing and a Facebook page.

## Interview guide — ask Roger these, then write it

Fifteen minutes on the phone produces a better story than anything inferable. Record
it and keep his phrasing; the best lines in a brand story are almost always the
client's own words, lightly trimmed.

**The decision**
1. What were you doing before Cathedral, and what made you decide to open your own place?
2. Was there a specific moment or a specific piece where you thought, I need to do this my way?
3. Why the name Cathedral? Who chose it, and what did you want it to mean?

**The work**
4. What kind of piece do you most want to be known for?
5. What is the piece you are proudest of, and what was the story behind it?
6. What do you do differently from how you were taught, or from how you see it done around here?
7. What do you refuse to do?

**The customer**
8. Tell me about a client who walked out and it clearly meant something to them.
9. What do people get wrong when they come in for a memorial or a portrait?
10. What do you wish people knew before they booked?

**The shop**
11. What do you want the new space to feel like when someone walks in?
12. Where do you want this in five years? One chair, or a roster?

**The governance question, asked plainly**
13. How should we describe your role? The state filing lists Mikayla Paul as the sole
    member of the LLC, so I want to get your title right rather than guess.
14. How do you want your previous shop handled, if at all? My default is not to
    mention it.

## Tone calibration — decided in advance

When the answers arrive, write it:
- **Founder-forward, first person.** Roger's own voice. The trade runs on the person,
  not the corporation, and the DM funnel means clients are already talking to him
  directly (`audience.md`).
- **Raw and plain over polished.** Per `voice.md`: the mark is gothic, the words are
  not. No liturgical register, no cathedral metaphors stretched past their welcome.
  One reference to the name's meaning, at most.
- **Pragmatic with restraint on emotion.** The subject matter is already heavy. The
  writing should not add weight; it should carry it steadily.
- **The customer is the hero.** Roger is the guide, not the protagonist.
- **Earned confidence, no chest-beating.** A four-month-old shop that writes like an
  institution is transparent. A four-month-old shop that writes plainly about the work
  reads as confident.
