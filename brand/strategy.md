## 07 — Brand personality

**Axis summary:** Serious · Warm · Considered · Traditional · **Restrained (premium)**

**Personality statement.** Cathedral is the quiet one in a loud category. It does not
announce itself, because the mark and the work already do; it would rather explain
what a piece will take than argue that it is the best in the parish. It is warm toward
the person and serious about the job, and it treats a tattoo as something that will
outlive the conversation about it. The single defining trait is the gap between how it
*looks* and how it *talks*: gothic, gold, architectural on the surface, and completely
plain the moment it opens its mouth. That gap is deliberate and it is the brand's
signature.

## 08 — Brand voice

## 10 — Positioning

**Narrative.** Denham Springs is saturated and the saturation is literal: three shops,
including Cathedral, sit within one mile of each other on Florida Ave SW. Every one of
them brands the same way, on the same three claims, over the same dark website with
the same grid of photos. The category has decided its customer chooses on trust and
portfolio alone, and brands defensively as a result. Meanwhile the customer for the
work Cathedral actually does is not choosing a shop at all: they are choosing a person
who can be trusted with a face, a name, or a date. Nobody in the parish is speaking to
that customer, and nobody is executing online well enough to be found by them.

**Positioning statement.** Cathedral is where people in Livingston Parish go when the
tattoo has to mean something: **premium commissioned work**, treated with the weight it
deserves.

**Premium is the sharpened form of this position (Clint, 2026-09-05)** and it is
defensible for a structural reason: Tattootopia claims authority through tenure, which
a four-month-old shop cannot contest, but **nobody local claims the selective,
expensive seat — because taking it means refusing volume, and every shop on Florida Ave
is built on volume.** Cathedral is small enough to refuse volume and good enough to be
worth waiting for. That is a real moat, and it closes as soon as the shop grows.
🚨 The word itself is never used: premium is expressed through restraint, selectivity
and craft evidence (`context.md`, `voice.md`).

🚨 **v2: the structural argument above has a hole in it.** It says Cathedral can take
the selective seat because it "is small enough to refuse volume". It is not refusing
volume. It is a walk-in shop with six-day hours and no minimum. The moat described is
one the shop has not chosen to dig. `positioning.md` §08 sets out the two honest
options; BSC recommends releasing the premium ceiling unless Roger commits to a
minimum and deposits (R-30).

**Working position until B-05 is decided:** *considered work, and a room that is easy
to walk into.* True under either outcome.

**Map.** *Convenience ← → Considered commission* against *Generic presentation ← →
Distinct point of view.* Safe Voyage and Burning Lotus hold convenience/generic;
Tattootopia holds considered/mildly-distinct; Faithful holds considered/distinct
through family and faith. **The considered/most-distinct corner is empty.** Full
mapping in `positioning.md`.

## 11 — How Cathedral stands out

> **A cathedral mark over cathedral work. The brand and the portfolio finally agree.**

Cathedral's difference is congruence, and congruence is unusually hard to copy. Any
shop can commission a gothic logo; a shop whose portfolio is not actually built on
memorial portraits, religious black and grey and fine-line detail cannot wear one
without looking borrowed. The mark is not a costume here, it is a description. That
is the whole strategy, and it is why the recommendation is to spend on the identity
system rather than on claims.

**v2 adds a second, customer-written difference: people are put at ease here.** It is
in three of five reviews ① and no competitor's brand claims it.

What it refuses is equally load-bearing. Cathedral does not compete on price, tenure
or review volume. ⚠️ v2: it now competes on hours whether it means to or not. It makes no claim
about sterility, licensing, ownership or experience, because none can be substantiated
(`context.md`). It does not answer the shop up the street. What is left is narrow and
honest: the work, the plan, the attribution, and the open dates. ⚠️ **This is a brand
promise with thin evidence behind it.** Reverence is easy to assert and slow to prove,
and the only thing that converts it from claim to fact is reviews from people who
experienced it. ⚠️ **v2: the reviews came, and they do not say "reverence". They say
"comfortable".** The strategy should follow the evidence: keep the weight in the work
and the mark, and let the words be about ease.
