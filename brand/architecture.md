# Brand Architecture — Cathedral Tattoo Studio

*2026-09-05. Formalizes the decision Clint made 2026-09-04 and extends it to cover
the artists being hired. Source tiers per `context.md`.*

Cathedral has no product portfolio. The real architecture problem is the one every
tattoo shop has and most handle by accident: **the shop's brand and the artists'
personal brands compete for the same surfaces.** Artists arrive with their own
handles, their own followings, and their own marks burned into their photos. Get
this wrong and the shop becomes a room that famous-ish artists pass through,
carrying their audience out the door when they leave.

Roger's RC monogram is on nearly every portfolio photo. They are actively recruiting
artists and piercers ②. So this needs deciding now, before there is a roster.

| Mark | What it is | Audience | Relationship | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cathedral lockup** | Gothic facade, rose window, cross; blackletter "Cathedral" over "· Tattoo Studio ·" | The shop's public | Primary | **Developing.** Months old, 217 followers, zero reviews ① |
| **CTS gold badge** | Gold gothic monogram | Same | Studio secondary | Minimal — little published use |
| **RC gothic frame monogram** | Roger's personal artist mark | Roger's own following (@rc_tattoos) | Artist attribution | **Strongest of the three in practice** — on ~97 portfolio photos ① |
| **"RC Tattoos" skull sketch** | Legacy mark | — | Retired | Legacy only |
| *(future artist marks)* | Whatever the new hires bring | Their own followings | **Undefined — this doc defines it** | Unknown |

The uncomfortable read: **RC currently has more visible equity than Cathedral**,
purely because it is stamped on the entire body of work while Cathedral has a
months-old page and no website. The decision below deliberately spends down that
asymmetry in the shop's favor.

## 02 — Architecture recommendation

**Model: Branded House, with artist attribution as a credit line — not a sub-brand.**

Cathedral is the brand. Artists are credited *inside* Cathedral's frame, the way a
gallery credits the painter: the gallery owns the door, the wall and the invitation;
the painter signs the canvas. Nobody confuses the two, and neither is diminished.

Why this and not an Endorsed model ("RC Tattoos, at Cathedral"): an endorsed
structure would ask a months-old shop with no reviews to lend credibility it does
not yet have, while handing its best surfaces to a mark that can walk. Why not House
of Brands: a single-location shop cannot fund two brands, and there is no reason to
own two positions in one small market.

**What this means in practice:** every surface the shop controls leads with
Cathedral. Every surface that shows a *specific piece of work* credits the artist
who made it. Those are different surfaces and they never trade places.

## 04 — Naming system

**Convention:** `Cathedral + [descriptor]`. Plain descriptors, no invented sub-brand
names. "Cathedral Piercing" if piercing becomes its own service line; never
"Sanctum" or "The Reliquary" or any second gothic coinage — one ecclesiastical
metaphor per company is a brand, two is a costume shop.

**Rules**
- The parent name appears in every shop-level name. No exceptions.
- Artists are named, not branded: "Roger Cazares", not "RC by Cathedral".
- Avoid the category clichés Denham Springs is already full of — Ink, Inked, Needle,
  Parlor, Studio X. "Cathedral" works precisely because it is not in that set.
- Do not build a second name off the prior shop's family or visual language.
- Tiering (Pro / Plus / Premium) does not apply and should never be introduced;
  tattoo pricing tiers by artist and by piece, not by brand.

**Decision tree for anything new**
1. Is this a service the shop sells? → `Cathedral + [plain descriptor]`.
2. Is this a person? → their real name, credited inside Cathedral's frame.
3. Is this a one-off (guest spot, flash event, apprenticeship)? → an *event* name
   under Cathedral, never a standing brand. The 6/28/26 grand-opening flash graphic
   is the right precedent ①.
4. Would it need its own logo to work? → then it is the wrong idea for this shop.

## 05 — Visual identity system

Branded House: **one visual system**, differentiated by nothing but content.

- **Logo relationship:** Cathedral only, in every chrome position. The CTS badge is
  permitted where the full lockup will not read — a circular social avatar, a stamp,
  embroidery, an aftercare sticker. It is a size adaptation, not a second brand.
- **Colour:** one palette, shared, no per-artist accent colours. See `DESIGN.md`.
- **Typography:** one family stack. Blackletter is reserved for the lockup and rare
  hero moments; it does not become an artist's personal display face.
- **Voice:** one voice, shop-wide. Artist bios are written in it. Artists remain free
  to post however they like on their *own* handles — that is theirs, and trying to
  govern it would be both impossible and a bad way to keep staff.

**The RC rule, stated exactly.** RC stays on Roger's portfolio photos. That is his
signature on his own work; it is normal in the trade, it is evidence of authorship,
and stripping it would be both pointless and insulting. It never appears as favicon,
header, footer, avatar, OG card, print, or signage. Where RC and Cathedral would
appear in the same frame as peers, Cathedral wins.

**Apply the same rule to every new hire on day one.** Their mark may sit on their own
work. It gets no chrome. Setting this in the hiring conversation costs nothing;
retrofitting it onto someone who has been posting under the shop's banner for a year
costs a relationship.
