## Primary customer problems

### Problem 1: "What if it comes out wrong? I can't undo it."

The person with a portrait, a memorial piece or a first large sleeve. They have looked at every shop without messaging one.

**Brand response:** Look at the work first. Every piece here is credited to the artist who made it, so you can see who does what. Then tell us what you're thinking about. No promise of likeness or outcome is made in any wording.

### Problem 2: "I've never done this. I don't know what happens."

The first-timer. They are afraid of looking foolish.

**Brand response:** First time, or nervous about it? Say so when you come in. We'll walk you through what happens before anything starts, and you can ask whatever you want. `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`. Beside it, a customer's words: "He was professional, patient, and made the whole experience comfortable from start to finish." Keely, Google review.

### Problem 3: "I don't want to be rushed, judged or talked out of what I want."

They have had a bad experience elsewhere, or expect one.

**Brand response:** It starts with what you want and why. You'll get a plain answer about what will work on skin and what won't. The response names no other shop and describes only Cathedral. `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`

### Problem 4: "I've got one I need covered."

They regret a piece and do not know whether anything can be done.

**Brand response:** Some can be covered and some can't. Bring it in and we'll tell you which. No guarantee, no before and after on a memorial piece, and nothing that mocks the old tattoo. `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`: "we'll tell you which" is a promise about the studio's practice.

### Problem 5: "When can I actually get in?"

They work shifts, or they have finally decided and do not want to chase anyone.

**Brand response:** Walk in, or book ahead. Monday to Thursday 10am to 6pm. Friday and Saturday 10am to 7pm. Closed Sunday. Where dates are shown: "Check open dates". Never "waiting list", never "by appointment", never a countdown.

## Message pillars

No claim travels without its proof. Each pillar is shown with a photograph, a verbatim quote or a published fact beside it. Where the proof is thin, it says so here, and the pillar's own wording stays marked.

### Pillar 1: The work is the proof

**Core idea:** Cathedral does not describe its skill. It shows it. Fewer pieces, larger, each credited to the artist who made it, so a person can find the kind of piece they want and judge it themselves.

**Proof:**

- The photographs in `assets/photos/`, listed in `assets/photos/PHOTO-MANIFEST.md`: portraits and memorial pieces, black and grey, fine line and dotwork, lettering, colour, cover-ups, sleeves and back pieces.
- Captions in the fixed form: "[piece], [placement]. Roger Cazares."
- "Quality is unreal" Tyler, Google review

**Strength of proof: strong for one artist, absent for the others.** Every photograph on file is Roger Cazares's work. Nothing is on file for Mikayla Paul or Cleo. Their work appears only as labelled photo slots, and no line implies the range shown belongs to all three.

**Useful headlines:**

**Limit:** never "all styles", "any style", "award-winning", "best", "experienced" or a count of pieces. Never caption a piece "healed" unless the manifest says so. Never show a licensed character. Never retouch a tattoo.

### Pillar 2: Planned, not improvised

**Core idea:** Large work gets a plan before anything is drawn: the size, the placement and how many sittings it needs. `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`: the steps of the process as the studio actually runs them.

**Proof:**

- Sleeves and back pieces in the photographs are work that takes more than one sitting. That is visible in the photographs.
- Nothing else. No customer has written about planning, and nobody at the studio has described the process.

**Strength of proof: thin.** The photographs show that large work is done. They do not show how it is planned. Every sentence about the process is an agency draft and stays marked until the studio confirms it.

**Useful headlines:**

- Planned, not improvised.
- Big pieces get a plan. (`CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`)
- Tell us what you're thinking about.
- You don't need to have it worked out.

**Draft process steps (`CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`, all four):**

1. Tell us what you're thinking about. A reference photo, roughly where you want it, and rough size. A screenshot is fine.
2. We'll talk it through. What will work on skin and what won't, and where it should sit.
3. You'll get a plan. Size, placement and the number of sittings, before anything is drawn.
4. Then we tattoo it. Large pieces run across more than one sitting.

**Limit:** no price, no rate, no number of hours, no deposit wording, no spacing between sittings stated as a rule, no healing time, no promise of outcome. Never "consultation required". Never "by appointment".
