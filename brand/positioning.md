## 01 — Category definition

- **Stated category:** tattoo shop. GBP "Tattoo shop", Facebook "Tattoo & Piercing
  Shop" ①.
- **Actual category — what the customer is really comparing:** not shops at all.
  **Artists.** The audience evidence says people choose a person whose healed work
  proves they can do the specific thing being asked for, then tolerate whichever shop
  that person works in ④. Every competitor brands as a *shop* and lists artists as an
  afterthought; the customer is doing the opposite.
- **Opportunity category:** **commissioned work.** Not "tattoo shop" but the place you
  go when the piece has to be right — closer to commissioning a portrait than to
  buying a service.

⚠️ **Premium reframes this (2026-09-05).** The opportunity category is not just
commissioned work but *commissioned work at the top of the local market*. That is a
narrower and more defensible seat: Tattootopia claims authority through tenure, which
Cathedral cannot contest, but nobody local claims the **considered, selective, expensive
** position — because doing so requires refusing volume, and every shop on Florida Ave
is built on volume.

**Recommendation: compete in "commissioned work", present artists first.** It matches
how the customer actually decides, it is the one framing no local competitor uses,
and it is the only category where a four-month-old shop can compete at all — because
commissioned work is judged on the portfolio, and the portfolio is substantial — 97
filed pieces including large multi-session sleeves and back pieces ① — while the LLC
is four months old. ⚠️ Draw that distinction carefully: **the body of work is
visible; the business is new.** The portfolio evidences skill, never tenure.
⚠️ Roger's actual years of experience are **unknown** — never state or imply a
number (guardrail 3).

## 03 — Positioning territory

**The Space.** Reverence. Cathedral is where a tattoo is treated as something with
weight — a commission, not a purchase. **Positioned at the top of the local market**
(Clint, 2026-09-05): the considered, expensive-feeling choice, expressed through
selectivity and craft rather than announced in adjectives.

**The Audience Owned.** The person carrying one specific piece they have thought
about for months — a memorial portrait, a religious scene, a tribute, a first large
sleeve — who is frightened of it being done badly.

**The Competitive Moat.** Three layers, in ascending durability. The mark itself is
the most distinctive in the parish and would cost a competitor a rebrand to answer.
Beneath that, the alignment is real: a cathedral mark over a portfolio genuinely
heavy in religious, memorial and portrait work is congruence, not decoration, and
congruence cannot be copied by a shop whose work does not match. Deepest, the
territory is *temperamental* — reverence is a way of running a room, and a shop built
on speed and volume cannot adopt it without dismantling its own economics.

## 05 — Proof points

Only what can be substantiated **today**. Each is marked with what it rests on.

1. **The portfolio is deep in exactly this work.** 97 filed photos ① — memorial
   portraits, religious black and grey, Bible-verse script, dog portraits, large
   sleeves and back pieces, fine-line dotwork. *Verifiable by looking.*
2. **The work spans multiple sittings and heals well.** Large-scale sleeves and back
   pieces in the portfolio are inherently multi-session ①. *Verifiable by looking.*
3. **Range without dilution.** Black-and-grey realism, fine line, dotwork,
   neo-traditional colour, lettering, cover-ups ① — breadth demonstrated in work, not
   claimed as "all styles."
4. **Availability published openly.** Roger posts month-by-month open dates and
   same-day walk-in openings ②. *Real behaviour, and the competitors do not do it.*
   ⚠️ v1 called this "appointment-led". The shop takes walk-ins; do not say that.
5. **The brand and the work agree.** The mark is ecclesiastical; the portfolio is
   religious and memorial-heavy. Rare in the category. ④
6. ✅ **People are made comfortable. NEW in v2, and the strongest proof here because
   customers wrote it.** Three of five readable Google reviews are about ease:
   "made the whole experience comfortable from start to finish" (Keely), "I am an
   anxious person so that's a big deal for me" (Alesha), "Got my first ever tattoo
   here... felt completely comfortable" (Hailey) ①.
7. ✅ **Customers come back to a named artist.** "i strictly go to Cleo for all my
   tattoos" (Kameryn); Alesha has had "a few" from Mikayla ①. Repeat custom inside
   five months of trading.
8. ✅ **Real money is already being spent.** Two reviewers disclosed $450–500 and
   $500+ ①. *Internal evidence for the price ceiling. Never published as a price.*
9. ✅ **6 Google reviews, 5.0, every one answered** ① (2026-10-11). Usable under the
   rewritten guardrail 5: exact count, exact date, verbatim quotes only.
10. ⏳ **Licensing and permits** — still unusable. LDH has no public registry; publish
   nothing until Roger sends numbers (guardrail 4).

**Proofs deliberately not used:** years in business (under five months), awards (none
known), sterility/licensing (unverified), "price friendly" (a customer's phrase and a
fair one, but repeating it would put Cathedral in a price conversation), "premier" (unfalsifiable
and Tattootopia's line), and **"premium" itself** — the position is demonstrated, not
asserted.

🚨 **R-07 answered 2026-09-05: there is no shop minimum.** This is the sharpest
tension in the package and it should not be smoothed over.

A minimum is a **filter, not a price.** Without one, the calendar fills with the
smallest, cheapest work, which consumes the chair time that large commissioned pieces
need and generates nothing for the portfolio. The positioning above — considered,
selective, unhurried, appointment-led — describes a studio that *chooses its work*. No
minimum describes a studio that takes what comes. Both are viable businesses; only one
of them is this one.

**BSC recommends instituting a minimum** ($100 market-standard, $150 to match the
position; local anchor ③), paired with a booking deposit. **Pricing is Roger's call.**
If he declines, premium must be carried entirely by consult-gating, deposits and
refusal of small work — achievable, but harder and less forgiving (`context.md`).

## 06 — What this brand refuses to be

- ~~Cathedral is **not for people who want a tattoo today.**~~ ❌ **Struck in v2.**
  Walk-ins are live and the studio calls itself a walk-in shop. What survives is
  narrower: Cathedral **does not sell itself on speed.** You can walk in. The pitch is
  still the work.
- Cathedral **will never compete on price.** It is positioned **premium** — the top
  of the local market, not a mid shop charging slightly more. Local evidence supports
  the ceiling: this market already pays above the cheapest option ③.
- Cathedral **will never say the word "premium."** ⚠️ Premium is expressed through
  restraint, selectivity, craft evidence and pace. Claiming it from a shop with zero
  reviews reads as pretension, and it would land as a direct answer to Tattootopia's
  "premier" (`context.md`).
- Cathedral **does not discount, run promos, or manufacture urgency.** ⚠️ Live tension:
  the grand opening was a flash event ① and the availability posts function as promos
  ②. Decide deliberately whether flash days continue.
- Cathedral **does not claim experience it cannot document.** No "20+ years", no
  "award-winning", no "established". The business is four months old (guardrail 3).
- Cathedral **is not the Christian tattoo shop.** Faithful holds that ground with
  real reviews. Cathedral is reverent, not denominational — the difference is that
  reverence extends to the customer memorialising a dog.
- Cathedral **does not define itself against the shop up the street.** No comparison,
  no mention, no prior-shop chapter in the story. ⚠️ BSC conflict — see
  `competitors.md` §07.

## 07 — Positioning in one sentence

**Cathedral is where people in Livingston Parish go when the tattoo has to mean
something — commissioned work, treated with the weight it deserves.**

## Red-flag check

Run against the skill's own red flags:

| Red flag | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Could apply to any competitor | **Pass.** No local competitor claims reverence or the commission framing; the two nearest occupy convenience and family-faith |
| Audience too broad to own | **⚠️ Weakened in v2.** v1 passed by excluding walk-ins. The shop takes them, so the audience is wider than the position admits. Still ownable if the lead stays on considered work |
| Differentiator is a table stake | **Pass.** Table stakes here are sterile/experienced/custom — all three are explicitly refused above |
| Cannot be substantiated | **⚠️ Partial, and premium raises the bar.** Proofs 1–4 are verifiable now; proof 5 is inference. A premium position demands *more* evidence than a mid-market one, and Cathedral currently has zero reviews and no price floor. **The position is right and currently under-evidenced** — a brand promise the shop must be seen keeping |

**Biggest risk to this position (v2):** no longer the absence of proof. Six reviews
exist and they are good. The risk is now **a gap between what the brand says and how
the shop runs** — a selective, reverent register over a walk-in shop a customer calls
"price friendly". Nothing in the six reviews supports "reverence"; all of it supports
"skilled and easy to be around". The mitigation is to say only what the shop does,
and to get B-05 decided before the site copy is written.

without being asked, which is the clearest statement of intent we have had from him.
Option B loses nothing that was real: the mark, the portfolio, the plain voice, the
named artists and the tagline all carry over unchanged. It only stops promising
selectivity nobody at the studio has agreed to practise.

**Until Clint rules, every doc in this package is written so that it is true under
both.** That is why v2 removes false claims and adds evidenced ones, and changes
nothing else.
