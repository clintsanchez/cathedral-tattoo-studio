## The binding limits

These apply to every sentence and every design made for the studio. If a line or a layout breaks one, it goes. The full register, with reasons, is at the end of file 6 and overrides this file.

1. No ownership language for anyone: no "owner", "founder", "owned and operated" or "family-owned". The only title is "Tattoo artist at Cathedral Tattoo Studio".
2. No tenure: no years, "since", "established", "experienced" or "veteran". No prior shop for any artist.
3. No licensing or hygiene claim in the brand's own voice: no "licensed", "certified", "sterile", "clean", "safe", "hospital-grade" or "health department approved".
4. No premium word: premium, luxury, high-end, elite, exclusive, bespoke, boutique, upscale.
5. No superlative: premier, best, top-rated, #1, award-winning, world-class, utopia.
6. No price: no figure, no "from", no minimum, no hourly rate, no deposit, no "affordable", no "price friendly", no discount, promo, sale, giveaway, countdown or "spots going fast".
7. No false booking model: no "by appointment", "appointment only", "booking required", "limited intake" or "waiting list". The wording is "Walk in, or book ahead."
8. No other business: no competitor's name, no comparison, no allusion to another shop.
9. No piercing in any layout. It is unconfirmed.
10. Nothing about a move: no "new location", "moving" or "bigger space", and no second address.
11. Reviews: only the dated rating line, "5.0 on Google from 6 reviews (October 11, 2026)", and only verbatim quotes from the approved list in file 5. None invented, paraphrased or tidied. None from the reviewer "hailey paul".
12. No religious framing: no "Christian", "faith-based", scripture used as marketing copy, or denominational language. Religious imagery appears only as tattoo work in photographs.
13. No category cliches: "ink" as a noun, "inked", "parlor", "body art", "bring your vision to life", "your body is a canvas", "no regrets", "journey", "transform".
14. No selling grief: no "honour your loved one", no "keep their memory alive", no before and after on memorial pieces.
15. No liturgical or gothic prose: no "sanctuary", "sacred", "hallowed", "ritual", "congregation" or "reverence" in copy.
16. Real photographs only, from `assets/photos/`. No stock, no AI-generated tattoos, skin, people, rooms or logos, no retouching of a tattoo.
17. The logo is the supplied raster, composited unchanged, on a dark ground, once per view. It is never redrawn, traced, recoloured or set in live type.

### The idea in brief

Two things are known about this studio and they pull in different directions on first sight. The mark is severe: a gold gothic cathedral on black. The photographs on file show work that carries weight: portraits, memorial pieces, large black and grey sleeves and back pieces, fine line and dotwork. Yet the customers who have written reviews do not write about weight. They write about being made comfortable, about a first tattoo, about being listened to.

The proposal is to hold both. The work leads every layout and is shown large, credited to the artist by name. The words are plain and at ease, so that the person who is nervous about walking in reads the page and finds nothing to be nervous about. The mark is gothic; the words are not. The door is open: walk in, or book ahead.

### The open decision B-05

The agency positioned the studio as premium in September 2026. Since then the live shop has published hours six days a week, takes walk-ins and has no minimum. Clint Sanchez of BlakSheep Creative has not ruled on whether the premium position is held or released. This kit does not resolve it. Everything here is written to be true under either answer:

- Commissioned work leads every layout. That holds either way.
- Walk-ins are stated plainly as a fact (hours, "Walk in, or book ahead."). They are never a headline, never a promotion.
- If premium is held, it is expressed through restraint: more black, fewer and larger images, space, exact words. It is never claimed in words.
- No copy promises selectivity the shop does not practise. No copy talks about price in either direction.
- The tagline "Work worth carrying" is about the work, not the booking model, and fits both outcomes.

Do not design a "premium" variant and an "accessible" variant. Design the one system that is true under both.

## Brand personality (`CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`)

Considered. Skilled. Plain-spoken. Welcoming. Steady.

A skilled tradesperson across a counter, telling you plainly what a piece will take. Warm toward the person and serious about the job. The defining trait is the gap between how the brand looks and how it talks: gothic, gold and architectural on the surface, completely plain the moment it speaks. That gap is deliberate.

"Welcoming" is the customers' word, not the agency's. It is the one personality trait with written evidence behind it.

For design, that means: black as the field and gold as gilding; a few pieces shown large instead of a wall of thumbnails; warm directional light, faces and the room to offset the severity of the mark; sentence case; generous space; no shouting, no starbursts, no skulls-and-flames costume, no second gothic face.

## Audience

The studio has two kinds of customer in view, and they want different things. One is inferred and one is evidenced, and the difference matters. Age, income and household make-up are not known and are not to be guessed in any output.

### Audience 1: the person with one piece that has to be right (inferred)

Someone in Denham Springs or the Baton Rouge area who has decided on a specific piece and has been carrying the idea for months: a memorial portrait, a large sleeve or back piece, a cover-up of something they regret. They research heavily, save references to their phone, and look at an artist's work for proof of the exact thing they want. They want a plan and an honest answer. They are choosing a person, not a shop.

**Basis: inferred.** This reader is read off the photographs on file, all of which show one artist's work. No customer has described themselves this way in a review, and nobody at the studio has confirmed it. Treat it as a working hypothesis.

### Audience 2: the first-timer or nervous client (evidenced)

Someone getting a first tattoo, or someone anxious about the whole thing who has been rushed or judged before. They are not choosing on depth of portfolio. They are choosing on whether the room feels easy. They want to know what happens and that nobody will hurry them.

**Basis: evidenced.** This reader is described in the studio's own Google reviews (read 2026-10-11), in customers' words: "I am an anxious person so that's a big deal for me" and "made the whole experience comfortable from start to finish". It is the only audience with written evidence behind it.

The two are not in conflict. Both want to be taken seriously: one for the piece, the other for themselves.

### How to speak to each

| Customer | Cares most about | Lead with | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| The memorial or portrait commission | Likeness, and not being rushed | Portrait work shown large, and what to bring: the clearest photo they have | Selling grief back to them. Any before and after. Any promise of likeness |
| The large-scale project | Cohesion across sittings, and dates that hold | Sleeves and back pieces from the photographs; that large work is planned across sittings (`CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`) | Vagueness dressed as mystique. Any price, rate or number of hours |
| The cover-up | Whether it can be done at all | An honest answer: some can be covered and some cannot, and the studio says which | A guarantee. A before and after on a memorial piece. Mocking the old tattoo |
| The first-timer or nervous client | Not feeling stupid, rushed or judged | One plain sentence, and a customer's own words | Reassurance cliches. Gothic framing anywhere near this message. Any hygiene claim |

### Customer priorities

- The piece looks the way they pictured it, or better.
- Somebody listens before anything is drawn.
- They are told plainly what will and will not work.
- Nobody rushes them.
- They know when they can get in.

### Customer concerns

- "What if it comes out wrong? I can't undo it."
- "I've never done this. I don't know what happens."
- "Will I be judged for what I want, or for the tattoo I already have?"
- Cost. People ask. The brand answers with what it needs to know to tell them (the piece, the placement, the size) and with "Call or text (225) 301-9527". Never with a figure or a claim.
- "When can I actually get in?"

### Who the brand is not for

- **Anyone seeking hate imagery or extremist iconography.** A gothic and religious visual language can attract misreadings. No layout, sample caption or placeholder leaves room for one.
- **The impulse and party crowd, as a pitch.** People walk in and the studio welcomes them. The brand never pitches impulse, matching party tattoos, dares or drinking. No "get tattooed tonight" energy.
- **The price shopper, as a pitch.** The brand never talks about price in either direction, so it never courts or rebuffs this reader.

- Not the Christian tattoo shop. Religious pieces are tattooed and photographed like any other work. The brand has no denominational voice.

## Design principles

1. **Black is the field.** About 83 per cent of any layout is the ground, `#0A0A0A` or darker photography. With eyes half shut, a layout reads as black.
2. **Gold is gilding.** 12 to 15 per cent at most. A layout that is half gold has misread the brand.
3. **The mark is gothic; the words are plain.** The logo carries all the drama. Type is sentence case and unornamented. No second gothic face.
4. **Fewer pieces, larger.** One piece at full width says more than nine in a grid.
5. **Offset the severity.** Warm directional light, faces, the room, generous space. The first-timer has to be able to read this page without flinching.
6. **Commissioned work leads; walk-ins are a fact.** The hero is a piece of work. "Walk in, or book ahead." sits with the hours.
7. **Real or nothing.** A tattoo, a person or a room on the page is a real photograph from `assets/photos/`. Where none exists, the space is black and type, or a labelled photo slot.
8. **Customers do the praising.** The brand states facts in its own voice. Adjectives arrive inside a verbatim review, with a first name and "Google review".
9. **Premium by restraint, never by word.** Space, pace and exactness. No adjective.
10. **One clear next step.** "See the work" and "Call or text (225) 301-9527".
