## 05 — Brand goals

Derivable, because the client's own posts state their operational intent ②. Ordered by
what unblocks the most.

**Get the identity system out of raster.** The vector logo blocks the favicon, the OG
card, print, signage and the real colour tokens. It is the highest-value ask in the
package and the cheapest to fulfil.

**Settle the record.** ✅ Address confirmed 2026-09-05. Ownership language and
licensing numbers remain, and until they clear, the About page, bios, story,
recruitment copy and every trust claim stay blocked.

**Exist in search.** No website, no reviews, one published day of opening hours ①.
Competitors have broken or absent websites (`competitors.md`), so competent execution
alone is a real advantage here.

**Generate the first reviews.** Zero against Burning Lotus's ~168 ③. This is the
largest single competitive gap and no amount of positioning closes it. It should begin
the week the address settles.

**Fill the new location.** The client's own stated intent ②, and the commercial reason
the rest exists.

**Recruit without diluting the brand.** They are hiring artists and piercers ②. Set
the artist-attribution rule at the point of hire, not retroactively
(`architecture.md`).

**Turn availability into the conversion mechanic.** Roger already publishes open dates
②. It is the best marketing asset the shop has and it currently lives only in a
Facebook feed.

## 07 — Brand personality

**Axis summary:** Serious · Warm · Considered · Traditional · Restrained

**Personality statement.** Cathedral is the quiet one in a loud category. It does not
announce itself, because the mark and the work already do; it would rather explain
what a piece will take than argue that it is the best in the parish. It is warm toward
the person and serious about the job, and it treats a tattoo as something that will
outlive the conversation about it. The single defining trait is the gap between how it
*looks* and how it *talks*: gothic, gold, architectural on the surface, and completely
plain the moment it opens its mouth. That gap is deliberate and it is the brand's
signature.

## 08 — Brand voice

> **Cathedral speaks with plain warmth — specific, careful, and unhurried.**

The register is a skilled tradesperson across a counter: contractions, short
sentences, no superlatives, no urgency. The governing rule is that the mark is gothic
and the voice is not — writing sepulchral copy to match the logo would make the brand
ridiculous, and Roger's own posts are already plain and direct ②.

## 10 — Positioning

**Narrative.** Denham Springs is saturated and the saturation is literal: three shops,
including Cathedral, sit within one mile of each other on Florida Ave SW. Every one of
them brands the same way, on the same three claims, over the same dark website with
the same grid of photos. The category has decided its customer chooses on trust and
portfolio alone, and brands defensively as a result. Meanwhile the customer for the
work Cathedral actually does is not choosing a shop at all: they are choosing a person
who can be trusted with a face, a name, or a date. Nobody in the parish is speaking to
that customer, and nobody is executing online well enough to be found by them.

**Positioning statement.** Cathedral is where people in Livingston Parish go when the
tattoo has to mean something: commissioned work, treated with the weight it deserves.

**Map.** *Convenience ← → Considered commission* against *Generic presentation ← →
Distinct point of view.* Safe Voyage and Burning Lotus hold convenience/generic;
Tattootopia holds considered/mildly-distinct; Faithful holds considered/distinct
through family and faith. **The considered/most-distinct corner is empty.** Full
mapping in `positioning.md`.

## 11 — How Cathedral stands out

> **A cathedral mark over cathedral work. The brand and the portfolio finally agree.**

Cathedral's difference is congruence, and congruence is unusually hard to copy. Any
shop can commission a gothic logo; a shop whose portfolio is not actually built on
memorial portraits, religious black and grey and fine-line detail cannot wear one
without looking borrowed. The mark is not a costume here, it is a description. That
is the whole strategy, and it is why the recommendation is to spend on the identity
system rather than on claims.

What it refuses is equally load-bearing. Cathedral does not compete on price, hours,
tenure or review volume, and three of those it cannot win today. It makes no claim
about sterility, licensing, ownership or experience, because none can be substantiated
(`context.md`). It does not answer the shop up the street. What is left is narrow and
honest: the work, the plan, the attribution, and the open dates. ⚠️ **This is a brand
promise with thin evidence behind it.** Reverence is easy to assert and slow to prove,
and the only thing that converts it from claim to fact is reviews from people who
experienced it. That is the strategy's central risk and it is operational, not
creative.

## 12 — Tagline suggestions

Five options are developed with style and rationale in `messaging.md`. Ranked:

1. **Work worth carrying** — *recommended.* States the position in four words, covers
   every persona, survives all seven guardrails, and does not date against the walk-in
   change.
2. **Take the time it takes** — captures the unhurried quality; slightly passive.
3. **For the ones that matter** — warm; ambiguous between people and pieces.
4. **Commissioned, not walked in** — sharpest, but ⚠️ expires when walk-ins go live.
5. **Black and grey, done right** — most searchable, but narrows the brand to one
   style.

The studio has no existing tagline to rate ①. ⚠️ Nothing is locked until Roger says it
out loud.
