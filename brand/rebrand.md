# Rebranding Assessment — Cathedral Tattoo Studio

*2026-10-11. First run of the `rebranding` skill. On 2026-09-05 it was marked "not
applicable" in `naming.md` and skipped. That was right then. It is worth running now
because the audit (v3) found real drift between the declared brand and the live shop,
and drift is what this skill exists to diagnose.*

> **Verdict: no rebrand. A positioning correction, before launch, costing nothing.**
> Cathedral has no public brand to change. No website, no published tagline, no
> printed material, no signage in the new identity. What has drifted is an internal
> document, not a public promise. Fixing it is an edit, and `positioning.md` §08
> already holds it as decision **B-05**.

---

## 01 — Rebrand diagnosis

**Why this is being asked (honest version).** Nobody asked for a rebrand. The
question arises because the brand package, written 2026-09-05, describes a premium,
selective, appointment-led studio, and the shop that has operated since is a walk-in
shop open six days a week whose customers praise comfort and call it "price
friendly". If the site launches on the September position, it will describe a
business customers do not find when they arrive.

**Type of rebrand:** none of the four. The nearest is **brand repositioning**, but
repositioning assumes a position the market already holds in its head. The market
holds none. This is a **pre-launch positioning correction**.

**What's driving it:**
- [ ] Brand has outgrown its identity
- [ ] Target audience has shifted — *no. It was mis-read, from one artist's portfolio*
- [ ] Entering a new market or category
- [ ] Competitive pressure
- [ ] Reputation issue
- [ ] Merger, acquisition, or spinout
- [ ] Leadership change
- [ ] Visual identity is dated
- [x] **None of the above: the documented brand was written before the shop's
  behaviour could be observed.**

---

## 02 — Brand equity audit

**What has equity**
- **The name and the mark.** Five months old, and already the most distinctive
  identity in the parish. On the GBP, the socials and every graphic the studio has
  made. Customers write "cathedral tattoo" in reviews.
- **The artists' names.** Roger (6,663 Instagram followers), and now Mikayla and
  Cleo, each named by a returning customer. This is where the real equity sits.
- **A 5.0 rating across six reviews**, every one answered.
- **Roger's availability posts.** A habit customers already recognise.

**What is working**
- The plain voice, live in review responses and owner posts.
- Gold on black, against a black-white-red category.
- Walk-ins and six-day hours. They were the shop's own choice and customers use them.

**What is broken**
- Nothing public. Internally: the claims "appointment-led", "does not contest
  convenience" and "not for people who want a tattoo today" — all removed in v2.
- Unresolved: the premium ceiling, with no minimum and no deposits under it (B-05,
  R-30).

**What is neutral**
- The CTS badge. Little published use; keep for stamps and stickers.
- The legacy "RC Tattoos" skull sketch. Already retired.

## 04 — The brand direction after the correction

**Positioning statement (working, true under either B-05 outcome).**
Cathedral Tattoo Studio does considered tattoo work in Denham Springs, in a room
that is easy to walk into.

**What changed**
- The shop is described as it runs: walk in or book ahead, six days a week.
- Comfort is named as a strength, because customers named it first.
- Three artists, not one.

**What stayed the same**
- Commissioned work leads. The portfolio is still the strongest proof.
- The mark is gothic and the voice is not.
- Premium, if it is kept, is expressed and never claimed.
- Cathedral fronts everything; artists are credited by name on their own work.

**Personality:** considered · skilled · plain-spoken · welcoming · steady.
*("Welcoming" replaces "restrained". It is the customers' word.)*

**Audience:** expanded, not shifted. The person with a piece that has to be right,
and the person who needs the room to be easy.

## 05 — Transition narrative

**For existing customers:** none needed, and writing one would be a mistake. They
have seen no brand that is now changing. Announcing a "new direction" to people who
walked in last month and liked it would invent a problem.

**For the people who work there** (to be said by Roger, not sent by us):

> We're building the website and tidying up how the shop shows up online. Nothing
> about how we work is changing. It'll say what's already true: good work, walk in or
> book ahead, and people feel comfortable here. Each of you gets credited by name on
> your own work. If a customer asks, that's the answer.

**For press / public:** not applicable. There is no rebrand story. The public moment
is the site launch, and that is a launch, not a relaunch.

## 07 — Risks

**Customer confusion — low.** Only arises if the site launches on the September
position. Mitigated by v2.

**Brand equity loss — low, with one exception.** Releasing the premium ceiling
(B-05 option B) gives up the empty "selective" seat in the local market. That seat
was never occupied, so nothing held is lost; an opportunity is.

**Internal misalignment — the real risk.** Roger has approved nothing: not the
position, not the tagline, not the voice (R-18, R-19, R-20). Three artists work
there and we have spoken to none. A brand the people in the room have not agreed to
will not be lived, whichever position is chosen.

**Market timing — favourable.** Correcting before launch is free. Correcting after
the site, the signage and the cards are printed is a rebrand.

**Execution — two gaps.** No work on file from Mikayla or Cleo (R-09), and no
print-ready logo art if signage is ordered.
